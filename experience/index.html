    Sixty-one speakers, one quiet pocket

    The puck is a flat phased array of 10 mm ultrasonic transducers running at 40 kHz, far above human hearing.

    Drag inside the field to move the focus. Each disc fires a fraction of a cycle early or late, so all 61 waves arrive at that point in step.

    The field is computed live from all 61 elements, each modelled as a piston source with a simple directivity term. Waves are shown 20,000 times slower than real.

    What it takes to hold a lantern

    FERRIN's body isn't a surface. It is a swarm of tiny beads, each held in its own acoustic trap and lit by lasers, retracing the lantern faster than your eye can follow.

    Drag toward one full frame and the racing beads fuse into a solid lantern. Beads are shown 60 times slower than real.

    Where the physics comes from

    FERRIN is fiction. The emitter physics, the particle speeds and the swarm limit come from published laboratory work. The scale-up to a whole lantern is our extrapolation, and the budget above shows exactly how far it reaches.

    1. Marzo A. et al. Holographic acoustic elements for manipulation of levitated objects. Nature Communications 6, 8661 (2015). Single-sided twin traps from a flat 40 kHz array.
    2. Smalley D. E. et al. A photophoretic-trap volumetric display. Nature 553, 486-490 (2018). Images drawn in air by a trapped, laser-lit particle.
    3. Marzo A. & Drinkwater B. W. Holographic acoustic tweezers. PNAS 116, 84-89 (2019). Up to 25 particles moved independently.
    4. Hirayama R. et al. A volumetric display for visual, tactile and audio presentation using acoustic trapping. Nature 575, 320-323 (2019). Trapped particle speeds up to 8.75 m/s.
